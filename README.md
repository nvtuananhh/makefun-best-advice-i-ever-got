## The entry

> My father said never lend money you can't afford to lose, then handed me his last two hundred dollars and called it tuition.

## Why it fits the brief

- **Most on subject:** it is literally a piece of advice, quoted from a father, passed down as a life rule.
- **Most earned:** the advice is not just spoken — it is immediately tested. He hands over his last two hundred dollars, the very money he warned about losing, so the lesson costs him something real.
- **Most alive as writing:** the twist lands in the final word. "Tuition" reframes a loss as an education, and the whole story — warning, gift, punchline — happens in a single sentence.
